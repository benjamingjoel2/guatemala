Guatemala packs an astonishing amount into a country roughly the size of England or Tennessee. In a single trip you can wander baroque ruins in the morning light, cross a lake beneath three volcanoes, watch an active volcano throw lava into the night sky and climb a Maya temple above the rainforest canopy. More than half of the population is Indigenous, and Maya languages, dress and traditions are woven into daily life rather than staged for visitors.

This is our guide to the places we think define the country, and how they fit together.

## Antigua: the colonial heart

Founded in the 16th century and capital of the Spanish colonial Kingdom of Guatemala until earthquakes in 1773 forced the government to move, [Antigua](/destinations/antigua) is a UNESCO World Heritage city of cobbled streets, pastel facades and open-sky church ruins. Three volcanoes frame the town: Agua, Acatenango and the restless Fuego.

It's the natural first stop for almost every itinerary. Spend your days exploring the ruins of La Recolección and the Capuchinas convent, visiting a coffee finca on the slopes outside town, or simply finding a courtyard café beneath the bougainvillea. The food scene is the best in the country, and the boutique hotels are some of the most beautiful in Central America.

**Don't miss:** sunset from Cerro de la Cruz, and the Santa Catalina Arch early in the morning before the crowds.

## Lake Atitlán: three volcanoes and a dozen villages

Aldous Huxley famously compared Lake Como unfavourably with [Lake Atitlán](/destinations/lake-atitlan), and once you see it you'll understand why. The lake fills a vast caldera at about 1,560 m, watched over by the volcanoes San Pedro, Tolimán and Atitlán.

Each village around the shore has its own character:

- **Santiago Atitlán** – a proud Tz'utujil town, home to the folk saint Maximón
- **San Juan La Laguna** – weaving and natural-dye cooperatives, painters and coffee
- **Santa Cruz La Laguna** – tranquil, steep and accessible only by boat
- **San Marcos and San Pedro** – yoga, cafés and a more bohemian scene
- **Panajachel** – the main gateway, with the best connections

The best way to experience the lake is from a private boat, stopping wherever you like. Sunrise from Indian Nose (Rostro Maya) is a short, steep hike with one of the finest views in the country.

## Chichicastenango and the highland markets

On Thursdays and Sundays the K'iche' Maya town of [Chichicastenango](/destinations/chichicastenango) hosts one of the most famous markets in the Americas. Stalls of textiles, masks, flowers and produce fill the streets, while on the steps of the Church of Santo Tomás, people burn copal incense in rituals that blend Catholic and Maya belief.

Further west, [Quetzaltenango](/destinations/quetzaltenango), known to everyone as Xela, is Guatemala's second city and a base for hot springs, highland hiking and smaller, untouristy markets.

## Tikal and the Maya world of Petén

If one site sums up the grandeur of the ancient Maya, it's [Tikal](/destinations/tikal). At its height, more than a thousand years ago, this was one of the most powerful cities in the Maya world. Today its temples rise out of a protected rainforest, alive with howler monkeys, toucans, spider monkeys and coatis.

We recommend staying close to the park so you can be inside early, or joining our [Tikal sunrise experience](/experiences/tikal-sunrise) to hear the forest wake from the top of a temple. Base yourself in the island town of [Flores](/destinations/flores-and-peten), and consider adding Yaxhá, with its lagoon sunsets, or for real adventurers, the multi-day expedition to [El Mirador](/destinations/el-mirador).

> A domestic flight from Guatemala City to Flores takes about an hour, and turns what would be a very long drive into an easy morning.

## Acatenango: watching a volcano erupt

Few experiences anywhere compare to a night on [Acatenango](/destinations/acatenango). From base camp at around 3,600 m you look straight across at Volcán de Fuego, one of the most active volcanoes on Earth, which often erupts every 15 to 20 minutes. At night the lava glows against the stars.

It's a demanding hike, but the reward is extraordinary. Our [Acatenango overnight hike](/experiences/acatenango-overnight-hike) is planned around your pace, with expert guides and porters.

## Semuc Champey and the Verapaces

Hidden in a jungle gorge in Alta Verapaz, [Semuc Champey](/destinations/semuc-champey) is a natural limestone bridge topped by a staircase of turquoise pools, while the Cahabón River thunders underground below. Add candle-lit swims through the Kan'ba caves and tubing on the river, and it's one of the most memorable days in Guatemala.

On the way, the cloud forests around [Cobán and the Verapaces](/destinations/coban-and-las-verapaces) offer orchid nurseries, cardamom and coffee farms, and the chance to see the resplendent quetzal, the national bird.

## The Caribbean: Río Dulce and Livingston

Guatemala's short Caribbean coastline feels like another country. A boat trip down the [Río Dulce](/destinations/rio-dulce-and-livingston) passes through a dramatic limestone canyon draped in rainforest before reaching Livingston, a Garifuna town reachable only by water. Try *tapado*, a coconut seafood soup, and listen out for punta drumming in the evening.

## The Pacific: black sand and turtles

For a few days of downtime, the Pacific coast offers wide black-sand beaches. At [Monterrico](/destinations/monterrico), you can explore mangroves by boat and, between roughly July and December, help release baby sea turtles at sunset.

## How to fit it all together

With **one week**, focus on Antigua and Lake Atitlán, perhaps with a highland market. With **ten days to two weeks**, add Tikal by domestic flight, and either Acatenango or Semuc Champey. With **three weeks**, you can take in most of the highlights at a relaxed pace, including the Caribbean or the cloud forest.

The secret is pacing. Roads are slow and winding, so we always plan routes that minimise long drives and give you time to linger.
